\section{Problem insight}
The released simulator builds every customer utterance from the target product's own catalogue record. Every spoken constraint is therefore a verbatim substring of the target document, and the task is string-provenance recovery rather than semantic similarity. Exact phrase matching is the estimator matched to that generative process.

Targets derive from real purchase records, so probability mass concentrates on heavily reviewed items. A review-count prior is consequently load-bearing in ranking. The population folds below bound what that bias costs.

Four transformer approaches were built and rejected on measurement: a dense bi-encoder, ColBERT late interaction, a cross-encoder reranker, and learning-to-rank over the candidate pool. Each degraded lexical precision. The learned components that ship decide what to keep or what a phrase means, never what matches.

\section{Method}
Escalation ladder. Exact mechanisms run first; learned components are reachable only when the exact ones produce nothing. The ordering is enforced by control flow, not by a confidence threshold.

\subsection*{Choosing the next question}
A reply signature is precomputed for every candidate and attribute from the frozen catalogue: the first two values the customer has not disclosed, which is what the simulator would say. Candidates sharing a signature are indistinguishable under that question, so signatures partition the pool into blocks of size $n_i$ over a pool of $N$. The agent asks the attribute minimising

\[ \mathbb{E}\,[\,|\text{pool after reply}|\,] \;=\; \frac{1}{N}\sum_i n_i^{2} \]
the expected size of the block a uniformly drawn target falls into. This is collision entropy rather than Shannon, since the cost is candidates left to rank. It reuses the pool the ranker is about to consume, reads only precomputed signatures, and falls back to a fixed order on any failure.

\code{category}, \code{brand} and \code{budget} are never asked: no evaluator branch can pay them out, measured at 0/200. That is a property of the simulator, not of the method. Nothing in the policy is specific to seven attributes; the criterion ranks whatever askable set it is given, so a deployment with more attributes, or a live shopper who can answer about budget and brand, simply widens the set. These three are excluded because the thing answering cannot reward them, and asking would spend a turn for a guaranteed zero.

Caveat on magnitude: every probe ordering lands within 0.003, inside the 0.0168 fold noise. The criterion is principled and free rather than measurably better at this sample size.

Constraints held across all generated corpora:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt,parsep=0pt,topsep=2pt]
\item Generator and solver are different model families. Paraphrases were not produced by the model the agent calls.
\item Wrapper banks split by template family, not by row: no test string appears in training in any form.
\item No corpus derives from a private set. Every proxy is resampled from the released catalogue.
\item Known weaknesses (for instance a small distinct-paraphrase pool) are recorded in the manifests rather than worked around.
\end{itemize}
The official set is 200 sessions from one popularity distribution with wording from one template family. It cannot separate a system that solved the task from one that fitted the sample. The population folds vary the target distribution; the paraphrase axes vary the wording.

Both learned checkpoints downloaded from the Hub into an empty cache and ran inference on an unparsed message.

\section{Model choice}
Local: two DistilBERT checkpoints, a dialogue-act router and a scaffolding tagger, published as KhiemGOM/techjam-route-classifier and KhiemGOM/techjam-scaffolding-tagger. Gitignored rather than committed, fetched from the Hugging Face Hub on first use. That fetch is the repository's only network dependency, and the README documents a pre-fetch command. Both load lazily and fail closed: absent torch, transformers or weights, the agent runs its standard-library lexical path unchanged.

Hosted: openai/gpt-oss-20b via Groq, used by the attribute deparaphraser and the transcript rescue. Both require GROQ\_API\_KEY, both are inert without one, and neither is required for any reported score.

Both hosted layers propose; the catalogue disposes. A recovered phrase becomes evidence only if it exists in the frozen catalogue, and enters at an attenuated tier rather than full constraint strength. Measured: identical proposals admitted at full weight recover 81.5\% of a perfect resolver, attenuated 96\%. The gap is the cost of wrong proposals.

Candidate values take one admission path regardless of producer: attest, else deparaphrase once, else drop. Previously each layer admitted evidence privately, and the rescue discarded 163 of its own proposals on the compound axis as unattested without offering them to the deparaphraser. Unifying the path is worth +0.0266 there.

On the scored set the two configurations are the same run. \section{Limitations}
\textbf{Simulator-shaped.} The verbatim-substring property belongs to the organizer's generative process, not to shopping. Against human phrasing the exact layers degrade, and the robustness evidence here is self-generated synthetic variation.

\textbf{Popularity-leaning.} The inverse-popularity fold bounds the cost of the ranking prior. A different demand distribution would need it re-calibrated; the agent self-calibrates its weight from label-free pool statistics, which limits but does not remove the exposure.

\textbf{Hosted calls are not bit-reproducible.} Even at temperature zero. Two independent runs on separate clones returned identical completions, but that is an observation, not a guarantee. All headline figures come from the offline path.

\textbf{Disclosure optimises the metric, not the experience.} One candidate per turn and ten on the last maximises MRR under the official formula; a shopper would prefer a shortlist throughout.
